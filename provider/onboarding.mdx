## What we need from you

Becoming a RouteMesh provider doesn't require any special software or SDK. We route consumer traffic to your RPC endpoints based on pricing, latency, and strategy — you just give us your endpoints and tell us what they support. Have the following ready before going live:

### Checklist

- **A RouteMesh account** — [routeme.sh/app](https://routeme.sh/app). If you already have a consumer account, we'll enable it as a provider. No dev work on your side.
- **Public RPC endpoint URL(s)** — the https endpoint(s) we send traffic to, per chain and region. Endpoints must be publicly reachable (loopback, private, and link-local hosts are rejected). If your endpoint takes an API key, put it in the URL — we route by full URL and don't support custom auth headers.
- **Chains you serve** — which chains (with chain IDs) your endpoints cover, and whether they're EVM.
- **Methods you support per chain** — the RPC methods each chain can handle (e.g. `eth_call`, `eth_getLogs`), including trace/debug if you offer them, and whether you serve archive data.
- **Node regions** — where your nodes are hosted (city/datacenter or cloud region). Latency from our gateways to your nodes drives how much traffic you win.
- **Pricing** — pay-as-you-go, priced per million requests (we can also price by method or compute unit). You can set different prices for economy vs performance routing, and per chain/method.
- **Capacity / rate limits** — how much throughput your endpoints handle (e.g. RPS). The rate-limit field in the portal is currently informational — use it to tell us your capacity, and set it to 0 if your nodes are effectively unlimited.
- **WebSocket endpoints (optional)** — if you serve `wss://` in addition to HTTP, include the websocket URL and its pricing.

We enable your account, then you add your **Nodes**, declare your **Methods**, and set your **Plans** in the provider portal. All three must line up for a route to be active.

## Step by step

### 1. Create your account

Start by creating an account at [**routeme.sh/app**](https://routeme.sh/app), or send us your org ID if you already have one.

### 2. Reach out

Email us at [**k@routeme.sh**](mailto:k@routeme.sh) — or reach us on [**Twitter / X**](https://twitter.com/routemesh) or [**LinkedIn**](https://linkedin.com/company/routemesh) — with the items in the checklist above. We'll flip your account to provider status and walk you through the rest.

### 3. Add your nodes and methods

In the **Provider portal**, register your RPC endpoint URLs (chain and region) and declare which methods you support per chain:

- [Nodes](/provider/nodes) — your RPC endpoint URLs
- [Methods](/provider/methods) — the RPC methods you handle per chain

### 4. Set your plans

Set your pricing per chain, method, and region. You can enter any price you want — how competitive you want to be is up to you. See [Plans](/provider/plans).

### 5. Go live

Once your nodes, methods, and plans are configured, your routes become eligible for consumer traffic immediately. There's no per-route approval step — the router selects you based on pricing and availability. You'll start seeing requests, market share, and revenue in the [Provider Dashboard](/provider/dashboard) right away.

## Getting paid

You earn revenue every time a consumer request is routed to one of your nodes. We settle on a billing cycle: you issue an invoice and we pay by USDC or card.

## What we handle

- **Routing** — We route consumer traffic to your nodes based on pricing, latency, and strategy
- **Monitoring** — You get a dashboard showing requests, market share, and latency
- **Failover** — Automatic retries across providers if any node fails
- **Data quality** — We run replay and lag checks to keep your nodes in good standing

See [Data quality](/provider/data-quality) for how checks work and what to do if your nodes flag failures.
