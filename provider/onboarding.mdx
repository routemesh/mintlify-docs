## How to join as a provider

Becoming a RouteMesh provider is straightforward. You don't need any special software or SDK — just your RPC endpoints and pricing.

### 1. Create your account

Start by creating a provider account at [**routeme.sh/app**](https://routeme.sh/app).

### 2. Reach out

Email us at [**k@routeme.sh**](mailto:k@routeme.sh) to let us know you'd like to join as a provider. You can also reach us on [**Twitter / X**](https://twitter.com/routemesh) or [**LinkedIn**](https://linkedin.com/company/routemesh).

We'll walk you through the next steps and get you connected.

### 3. Add your nodes and methods

Once you're in the **Provider portal**, you'll configure three things:

- **Nodes** — Register your RPC endpoint URLs with the chain and region they serve
- **Methods** — Declare which RPC methods (e.g. `eth_call`, `eth_getLogs`) you support per chain
- **Plans** — Set your pricing per chain, method, and region. You can enter any price you want — we don't force anything. It's up to you how competitive you want to be.

All three must align for your routes to become active and receive traffic.

See the following pages for details:

- [Plans](/provider/plans) — Pricing configuration
- [Methods](/provider/methods) — RPC method support
- [Nodes](/provider/nodes) — RPC endpoint management

### 4. Go live

Once your nodes, methods, and plans are configured, your routes become eligible for consumer traffic immediately. There's no manual approval step for individual routes — the router selects you based on your pricing and availability.

You'll start seeing requests and revenue in the [Provider Dashboard](/provider/dashboard) right away, including your market share and how you stack against other providers.

## What we handle

- **Routing** — We route consumer traffic to your nodes based on pricing, latency, and strategy
- **Monitoring** — You get a dashboard showing requests, market share, and latency
- **Failover** — Automatic retries across providers if any node fails
- **Data quality** — We run replay and lag checks to keep your nodes in good standing

See [Data quality](/provider/data-quality) for how checks work and what to do if your nodes flag failures.
